# Transfer-Pathway-Analysis

**Project Overview**
---

This project evaluated the effectiveness of a transfer pathway program by comparing academic performance, enrollment outcomes, and progression trends between program-affiliated transfer students and non-program transfer students. Using multi-year enrollment and performance data, I conducted a comparative trend analysis and developed executive-level reports to assess whether participation in the transfer pathway was associated with improved student outcomes and to identify opportunities for enhancing transfer student success.

**Question**
---

Do program-affiliated transfer students perform better than non-program transfer students, and what factors may influence differences in academic and enrollment outcomes between the two populations?

**Project Objectives**
---

- Compare academic performance outcomes between program and non-program transfer students.
- Evaluate enrollment, retention, and progression trends across multiple transfer student cohorts.
- Examine demographic characteristics and equity-related outcomes within transfer populations.
- Identify factors associated with student success, including transfer shock, registration eligibility, and credit accumulation.
- Assess whether participation in the transfer pathway program was associated with improved student outcomes.
- Provide data-informed recommendations to strengthen transfer student success initiatives and pathway partnerships.

**Data Sources**
---

- Student enrollment records
- Academic performance data
- Transfer student cohort records
- Registration and progression data
- Financial aid and unmet need data
- Student demographic information

**Methodology**
---

**Data Collection**

- Collected historical enrollment and academic performance data for three cohorts of Florida College System transfer students.
- Segmented students into program-affiliated and non-program transfer populations.
- Standardized enrollment, demographic, financial aid, and academic performance variables for comparison across cohorts.

**Data Analysis**

- Conducted a multi-year comparative trend analysis examining academic performance and enrollment outcomes.
- Evaluated GPA trends, graduation rates, registration eligibility, transfer shock, major changes, and credit accumulation.
- Analyzed demographic representation and financial need across transfer student populations.
- Identified relationships between academic performance and student progression indicators.

**Reporting and Visualization**

- Developed interactive visualizations and executive reports using Excel and Canva.
- Created comparative dashboard reports highlighting differences between transfer student populations and multi-year cohorts.
- Presented findings and recommendations to senior leadership.

**Tools Used**
---

- Excel
- Tableau
- Canva
- SPSS

**Key Findings**
---
**Analysis of three cohorts revealed that non-program transfer students generally demonstrated slightly higher academic performance, with performance differences ranging from approximately 1% to 7% across several key measures.**

- Enrollment declined by approximately 16% across both transfer student populations during the analysis period.
- Pell-eligible and First-Generation student representation remained relatively similar across both student groups.
- Black/African American students experienced approximately $9,000 in average unmet financial need.
- Hispanic/Latino representation generally increased or remained stable among higher-performing student groups.
- Higher GPAs were consistently associated with improved student outcomes, including increased graduation rates and registration eligibility.
- DFW rates generally declined as GPA increased.
- Approximately 50% of students with an ineligible registration status also had registration holds, suggesting a potentially significant barrier to continued enrollment.

**Business Recommendations**
---

- Expand advising and academic coaching supports to reduce transfer shock and support students during the transition to the university.
- Develop proactive outreach and intervention processes to identify students with registration holds before they become enrollment obstacles.
- Evaluate opportunities to reduce unmet financial need, particularly among student populations experiencing disproportionate financial challenges.
- Leverage predictive indicators such as transfer shock, GPA trends, registration eligibility, and credit accumulation to support targeted advising interventions.

**Analytical Limitations**
---

- The analysis identified differences between program and non-program transfer students but could not establish a direct causal relationship between pathway participation and student outcomes.
- Differences in student characteristics prior to transfer may affect academic outcomes independent of pathway participation.
- Historical trends may not fully predict future student outcomes due to policy changes, enrollment shifts, economic conditions, or evolving transfer student demographics.
- Measures such as transfer shock and registration eligibility provide insight into progression patterns but do not independently explain the underlying causes of those outcomes.
- Because the analysis examined institutional data from a limited number of cohorts, findings should be interpreted within the context of the student populations included in the study.

**Conclusion**
---

This analysis evaluated whether participation in a transfer pathway program was associated with improved academic and enrollment outcomes among Florida College System transfer students. While non-program transfer students generally demonstrated slightly stronger academic performance across several measures, both populations exhibited similar demographic characteristics and faced comparable challenges related to enrollment, transfer shock, and student progression.

The analysis identified actionable opportunities to improve transfer student outcomes through enhanced transition support, proactive intervention strategies, and targeted efforts to address financial and administrative barriers. Ultimately, the project provided leadership with evidence-based insights to inform future student success initiatives and strategic enrollment planning.
